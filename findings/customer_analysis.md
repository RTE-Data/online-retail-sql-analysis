### Customer Purchase Frequency Distribution

Customer purchasing behaviour is strongly concentrated among lower-frequency customers. **3,467 customers made 1–5 purchases**, compared with **535 customers making 6–10 purchases**, **242 making 11–20**, **80 making 21–50**, and just **15 customers making more than 50 purchases**.

The distribution therefore has a long tail, with a relatively small number of customers making purchases very frequently. Despite their small numbers, these high-frequency customers contribute substantially more revenue per customer.

### Customer Purchase Frequency and Revenue

Customer revenue increases substantially with purchase frequency. Customers making **1–5 purchases generated an average of approximately £867** in revenue, compared with **approximately £80,547** for customers making more than **50 purchases**.

This difference appears to be driven primarily by purchasing frequency and basket size rather than substantially higher item prices. **Revenue per item remained relatively stable, ranging from approximately £1.66 to £1.85** across the customer groups, while both the number of invoices per customer and items per invoice increased among higher-frequency customers.

The **50+ invoice group averaged 91.3 invoices per customer and approximately 471 items per invoice**, compared with **2.15 invoices and 240 items per invoice** for the 1–5 group. This indicates that the substantially higher revenue generated by frequent customers is associated primarily with repeated purchasing and larger baskets rather than substantially higher item prices.

Overall, the analysis shows a strong relationship between repeat purchasing behaviour and customer revenue, with a relatively small group of high-frequency customers contributing substantially more revenue per customer.
